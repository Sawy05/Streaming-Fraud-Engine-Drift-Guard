\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The exponential expansion of digital payments, instant settlement networks, and cross-border digital financial services has drastically heightened the urgency for automated real-time fraud mitigation systems. Fraud syndicates continuously evolve techniques—such as rapid card-testing bursts, synthetic account generation, and distributed account takeover (ATO)—inflicting severe financial and reputational damage on institutions worldwide.

Operating machine learning models in modern transaction environments entails rigorous constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sub-15ms Latency Budgets:} Card networks and payment gateways enforce tight authorization timeouts, allocating at most 10--20\,ms to fraud scoring.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Class Imbalance:} Fraudulent events represent a tiny fraction of total transactions ($\sim 1.5\%$), rendering standard symmetric loss functions ineffective.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Consistency:} Training pipelines must eliminate lookahead feature leakage by ensuring point-in-time consistency.
    \item \textbf{Continuous Non-Stationary Drift:} Adversarial adaptation induces rapid covariate and concept drift, necessitating real-time statistical observability.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges comprehensively, this work introduces \textbf{Streaming Fraud Engine \& Drift Guard}, a modular, high-throughput MLOps pipeline.
